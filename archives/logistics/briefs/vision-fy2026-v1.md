# Logistics Vision — FY2026

**Author:** Tejas Bhalerao | **Date:** 2026-08-13 | **Status:** Draft v1

---

## Where We're Going

By end of FY2026, Truemeds Logistics will be a competitive weapon — not a constraint.

Today, logistics is a source of silent conversion loss. Customers see a slow promise and don't check out. They get a broken promise and don't come back. We don't always know either happened. This year, we change that.

The goal is to make logistics an active driver of growth: a faster, more reliable, and more cost-efficient operation that wins customers at checkout and keeps them after delivery.

---

## Why This Year

The competitive window is narrowing. Quick commerce players have reset customer expectations on delivery speed. In pincodes where a competitor offers same-day delivery and we don't, we lose conversion silently — the customer never reaches checkout. In pincodes where we offer SDD but miss the promise, we lose trust.

FY2026 is the year we close both gaps.

We have the data, the network foundation, and the team to move from reactive ops to proactive, intelligence-driven logistics. The levers are identified. The sequencing is clear. What this year needs is focused execution.

---

## The Four Pillars

### 1. Promise — Win the checkout moment

**The bet:** A faster, accurate promise converts more customers than any other single lever available to us.

We will expand SDD to high-traffic, competitor-active pincodes, rationalise cutoffs to maximise speed, and build the intelligence layer (competitor ETA, promise elasticity) to continuously tighten what we quote. Where our network doesn't reach today, we'll identify where to invest so it does tomorrow.

**How we'll know it's working:** Average promise drops. Conversion rate in SDD-active pincodes rises.

---

### 2. On-Time Delivery — Deliver what we promise

**The bet:** A promise is only as good as our ability to keep it. At 51.4% OTD today, there's no other lever with this much upside.

We will fix the failures we own first — warehouse dispatch accuracy, courier selection intelligence, and address quality — before layering on customer-facing interventions. The path from 51% to industry-competitive OTD runs through data and operational discipline, not customer comms.

**How we'll know it's working:** On-time delivery rate climbs steadily across H1 and H2. NDR rate falls.

---

### 3. Delivery Experience — Execute the last mile correctly

**The bet:** Logistics doesn't own the customer relationship — but it owns every input that determines whether the customer receives their order and trusts us enough to order again.

We will build the handover infrastructure that makes last-mile execution verifiable: digital POD, geo-validated attempt detection, proactive status publishing, and CSAT capture. This is the foundation that makes the rest of the system legible — to CX, to ops, and to us.

**How we'll know it's working:** Digital POD rate rises. Mis-delivery rate falls. We have a delivery CSAT signal for the first time.

---

### 4. Cost — Build a sustainable logistics engine

**The bet:** SDD is loss-making below a density threshold. As we expand, cost discipline at the route, supply, and driver level determines whether the expansion is good business.

We will deploy DMS to optimise SDD route planning, supply-demand matching, and driver utilisation. The goal is not the cheapest logistics — it's the right cost structure for the volume we're building toward.

**How we'll know it's working:** Cost per delivered order trends down as SDD volume grows.

## How We Get There

### H1 — Fix the foundation
**Promise:** SDD expansion to qualified pincodes. Cutoff rationalisation. Network optimisation.
**OTD:** Warehouse and dispatch OTD. Courier selection intelligence.

This half is about eliminating the failures we already know about. Every day we delay costs us conversion and trust.

### H2 — Build the advantage
**Promise:** Ideal and consistent ETA. Network expansion planning. Serviceability coverage.
**OTD:** Address quality. Customer availability signals. DE behaviour monitoring.
**Experience:** DMS rollout — digital POD, handover signals, CSAT capture.
**Cost:** Route planning, supply planning, driver utilisation via DMS.

This half is about building the intelligence and infrastructure that makes FY2027 even stronger.

---

## What This Is Not

This is not a plan to fix everything at once. The levers are sequenced deliberately — OTD improvements require address quality before DE behaviour can be acted on. DMS is H2 because route optimisation at scale requires the network foundation H1 builds.

Depth on each initiative, owner accountability, and detailed metrics live in the Logistics AOP. This document is about direction and prioritisation — the decisions that determine what we do first and why.

---

## The Ask

Alignment on three things:

1. **The four pillars are the right bets for FY2026** — Promise, OTD, Experience, Cost in that order of sequencing.
2. **H1 is fully resourced** — SDD expansion, courier intelligence, and WH/dispatch OTD are underway or ready to begin.
3. **H2 dependencies are unblocked early** — DMS procurement, engineering capacity for the demand experiment, and Finance involvement for cost modelling all need to move in H1 to land in H2.
